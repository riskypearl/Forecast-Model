# Civic Ledger

**Philosophy:** Information as public record. The composition behaves like a carefully kept
ledger: a single sheet on which a whole contest is settled into rows, weights and one decisive
figure. Nothing decorative survives unless it carries meaning; everything that remains is
placed with the patience of a master typesetter.

**Space and form.** A warm paper field holds a strict vertical spine. Every row hangs from
that spine, and quantities grow outward from it as thin horizontal strokes, so the eye reads
balance before it reads numbers. Generous margins frame the sheet; the gutters are measured,
not guessed, and the result should feel meticulously crafted, as though each spacing decision
was revisited many times.

**Colour and material.** Two inks and a near-black. A deep civic blue and a brick red do all
the signalling; a graphite tone carries structure; a muted grey whispers secondary facts. The
palette is restrained so that colour is information, never ornament. The paper tone keeps the
piece tactile, like an object printed and pinned rather than rendered.

**Scale and rhythm.** One monumental numeral dominates, condensed and architectural, the
way a poster announces a single verdict. Below it the rhythm drops to a steady pulse of equal
rows: small caps, a quiet serif for names, monospaced figures aligned to the decimal. The
contrast between the shout and the steady murmur is the whole composition, calibrated with
painstaking attention.

**Composition and hierarchy.** Read top to bottom: verdict, proportion, evidence, source. A
strip of identical tiles shows the whole as parts before the rows show the parts as margins.
Where the two colours meet, a hairline marks the point of balance. Text is sparse and
essential - labels, never sentences - and every element sits clear of every other, the work
of someone at the very top of their craft.
